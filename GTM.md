# Go-To-Market Strategy

## Exact Target User

Not "startups." The specific person is:

**Engineering Manager or CTO at a 5–25 person B2B SaaS startup,
Series A or bootstrapped, burning $500–5000/month on AI tools across
their dev team, who has never audited that spend because no tool
existed to do it.**

They approved the Cursor Business plan 8 months ago when the team
was 3 people. Now the team is 8 and nobody checked if the plan still
makes sense. They're not irresponsible - they're just busy, and AI
tool pricing is opaque and changes constantly.

Secondary user: indie hackers and solo founders paying for 4-5 AI
tools simultaneously without knowing if they overlap.

## What They Google Before Wanting This Tool

- "cursor vs github copilot 2026"
- "is claude pro worth it for developers"
- "chatgpt team plan vs individual"
- "reduce saas spend startup"
- "ai tools budget engineering team"
- "copilot vs cursor for small team"

These are comparison and justification searches - someone about to
make a purchasing decision or questioning an existing one. This is
exactly when the audit tool is most useful.

## Where They Hang Out Online

- **r/SaaS** and **r/startups** - founders discussing tool costs
- **r/ExperiencedDevs** - engineers evaluating tooling
- **Hacker News** - Show HN is the single best launch channel
- **Indie Hackers community** - solopreneurs tracking every dollar
- **X/Twitter** - CTOs and engineering managers follow AI tool
  accounts; threads about "my AI stack" go viral regularly
- **Slack: Ramen Club, Online Geniuses, Demand Curve** - founder
  Slack groups where tool recommendations spread fast
- **Discord: Buildspace, theo.gg server** - developer communities
  actively discussing AI tooling

## First 100 Users in 30 Days - $0 Budget

**Week 1: Seeding**
- Post on r/SaaS: "I built a free tool that audits your AI tool
  spend - found $340/mo in savings on my own stack"
- Post on r/ExperiencedDevs with a specific scenario:
  "GitHub Copilot Business for 3 devs is $57/mo. Individual plans
  would be $30/mo. Built a tool that catches stuff like this."
- Submit to Hacker News as Show HN on a Tuesday morning
  (peak traffic window)
- Share in Indie Hackers "What are you working on" thread

**Week 2: Direct outreach**
- Cold DM 20 CTOs on X who have tweeted about AI tool costs
  in the last 30 days. Message: "Built something you might find
  useful - audited my own stack and found $200/mo in waste.
  Free tool, no login. Mind if I share?"
- Post in 3 founder Slack groups with a specific savings example,
  not a generic "check out my tool" message

**Week 3: Content**
- Write one Twitter thread: "We audited 50 AI tool stacks.
  Here's what we found." Use real anonymized data from early users.
- Submit to newsletters: TLDR, Pointer, Software Lead Weekly -
  these have submission forms and regularly feature free dev tools

**Week 4: Referral loop**
- The shareable audit URL IS the distribution - every user who
  shares their results is a free acquisition. Optimize the Open
  Graph preview to show the savings number prominently:
  "I'm saving $340/mo on AI tools - get your free audit"

## The Unfair Distribution Channel

Credex already has relationships with startup founders who buy AI
credits. Every existing Credex customer is a warm lead for the audit
tool - and every audit user who sees significant savings is a warm
lead for Credex credits. The audit tool and Credex credits are
each other's distribution. No competitor can replicate this loop
without also being Credex.
